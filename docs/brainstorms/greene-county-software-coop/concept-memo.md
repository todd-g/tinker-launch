# Concept Memo — Keep Greene County's Software Dollars in Greene County

*A member-owned co-op that replaces the out-of-county software subscriptions local businesses pay
every month — at cost.*

**The problem.** Greene County's small businesses send money out of the county every month — to
Square, Toast, Squarespace, Mailchimp, QuickBooks, and dozens of other out-of-state software
vendors. For a county whose economy runs on micro-businesses (the large majority have fewer than
10 employees), those recurring subscriptions and per-transaction fees are a steady, invisible drain.
None of it recirculates locally.

**The idea.** A nonprofit / cooperative shared-software service, owned by and accountable to its
member businesses. It takes mature open-source software, hosts it locally, and offers it to rooted
Greene County businesses **at cost** — not for profit. We don't build from scratch; we adapt proven
open-source tools (online ordering, scheduling, email, websites) and provide the one thing national
vendors never will: **in-person setup and support from neighbors.**

**Who it serves.** Rooted, owner-operated businesses that employ local people and keep their profits
here — the Chamber's members. Explicitly *not* absentee operators or value-extractors.

**Why it works now.**
- **The local multiplier:** dollars spent with locally-owned vendors recirculate in-community far
  more than dollars sent to national chains.
- **The onboarding moat:** a local co-op can sit at the counter and set it up — the decisive
  advantage with non-technical owners.
- **The money exists:** state (NY CFA / ESD feasibility grants) and federal (USDA Rural Business
  Development) programs fund exactly this kind of feasibility work and rural co-op development.

**The plan (de-risked, phased).**
1. **Survey & market study** — quantify how much money leaves the county, by software category.
   *(Free via SBDC + grant-funded.)*
2. **Discovery interviews** — 15–30 businesses: what they'd switch, what they'd pay, what they need.
   Pick the first target.
3. **Prototype & pilot** — adapt-and-host one tool for 3–5 pilot businesses; prove the savings;
   publish results.

**What we're asking of the Chamber / IDA.**
- **Endorsement & member access** for the survey (your credibility is the project's fuel).
- **Fiscal sponsorship / co-applicant** on grant applications (the IDA, EDC, or a 501c3 as the
  eligible applicant).
- A seat at the table shaping it.
